Metlink Synth

Wellington's buses, trains and ferries, as a generative ambient synth.

Buses hum

Every busy route is a drone: two detuned saws through a lowpass, on its own note of a just-intoned D pentatonic (longer route, lower note; other scales live in the settings). Each stop arrival plucks a little 2-op FM note at the moment it happened. The site runs 60 seconds behind live. This is so we can buffer and smooth events and also because metlink is never on time.

Trains buzz

Each train gets an inverter whine that climbs the scale as it speeds up, bit-crushed clacks at its movement rate, and a low buzz as it pulls into a station.

Ferries glorp

Each boat is a low tone with slightly seasick vibrato, pushed through a band-pass an LFO sloshes up and down. Docking lets the granular effect bark.

Everything modulates everything

Sorta. Routes sharing a road FM each other. Late routes drift flat and beat against themselves; early ones go sharp and bright. Speed opens the filter and sets the tremolo rate, so a traffic jam should go dark and still.

Turnarounds glitch

When a vehicle reaches the end of the line, a granular AudioWorklet grabs it and rewinds it into reversed, stuttering grains. The later the city is running, the more it feeds back.

You are the crosshair

You listen from the middle of the screen, and the distance rolloff fades sound out towards its edges: what you're looking at is what you hear. Zoom in and the street gets close, dry and a bit grubby with a touch of drive. Get within about 4 km and every bus on screen gets its own voice: heading east bends it sharp, west bends it flat, and its speed opens the filter. Zoom out and the whole region melts into reverb.

Why?

I like weird noises. I like data. I like civic infrastructure, and generative visuals and audio that are intrinsically linked at the source. This is built on an app I wrote ages ago, and the UI was brutally disciplined into shape by Claude. No rights reserved: do whatever the hell you want with it.
